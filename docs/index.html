01 · Profile

Research at the interface of membrane biophysics and scanning probe microscopy

I am an Inserm researcher in Marseille. My work focuses on the physical principles that govern membrane organization, dynamics and mechanical stability.

In our experiments, we image biological membranes and molecular assemblies directly in liquid. High-speed AFM allows us to resolve structural changes as they occur and to connect molecular organization with membrane deformation.

We complement imaging with force spectroscopy to quantify stiffness, breakthrough and rupture. Together, these approaches provide a direct view of membrane behavior across space, time and force.

02 · Research programme

Connecting molecular dynamics with membrane mechanics

We combine time-resolved imaging with quantitative force measurements to examine how membrane composition and molecular assemblies produce changes in shape and mechanical response.

2.1

Time-resolved imaging

High-speed atomic force microscopy

We record molecular motion and membrane reorganization in liquid at nanometre resolution. HS-AFM enables us to examine assembly, turnover and conformational change over experimentally relevant timescales.

2.2

Dynamic organization

Membrane remodeling

We investigate how proteins and lipids generate, stabilize and release membrane curvature. ESCRT-III provides one model system within our broader study of the mechanisms that couple molecular organization to membrane shape.

2.3

Quantitative mechanics

Membrane nanomechanics

We use force spectroscopy and high-speed force measurements to quantify bilayer deformation and rupture. We examine how lipid composition, phase behavior, ions and environment influence mechanical resistance.

Research question

How do molecular organization and lipid composition govern membrane dynamics and mechanical response?

We address this question by integrating HS-AFM movies with nanomechanical measurements on related membrane systems.

03 · Open source

Software for membrane biophysics and nanoscale mechanics

We develop open research tools that make computational workflows and lipid data easier to inspect, reproduce and extend.

2026 Python · finite-element analysis

Nanoindent

Nanoindent is an open-source Python alternative to commercial finite-element simulation workflows. It supports the simulation and analysis of AFM nanoindentation in thin elastic films and is validated against analytical contact-mechanics models.

Project documentation ↗
2026 Lipidomics · interactive atlas

LipidHub

LipidHub is an interactive, navigable atlas of the known and predicted lipidome. It brings curated and computationally inferred lipid species into one place, allowing users to search, compare and explore their structures and physicochemical properties.
