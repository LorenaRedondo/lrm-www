01 About

Lorena is a researcher at Inserm in Marseille whose work sits at the crossroads of biophysics, membrane biology and scanning probe microscopy.

She is an expert in atomic force microscopy (AFM) and high-speed AFM (HS-AFM), techniques that allow individual biomolecules and membranes to be imaged in liquid, at nanometre resolution and, with HS-AFM, in real time. She combines imaging with force spectroscopy to link how biological structures look, move and resist force.

Her research has addressed how ESCRT-III protein assemblies deform and remodel membranes, how lipid bilayers respond mechanically to their composition and environment, and how fast force measurements reveal the physics of membrane rupture.

02 Research

High-speed AFM

Video-rate imaging of single molecules and supramolecular assemblies in near-physiological conditions, capturing dynamics that static structural methods miss.

Membrane remodeling

How ESCRT-III polymers assemble, turn over and exert force on membranes, from spiral springs to Vps4-regulated filament dynamics, visualised directly by HS-AFM.

Nanomechanics & force spectroscopy

Phase behaviour, ion effects and rupture of supported lipid bilayers probed by force spectroscopy, including high-speed force spectroscopy of bilayer breakthrough.

Methods & community

Reviews and protocols on high-resolution and high-speed AFM, and involvement in the European Phys2BioMed network on physics for biomedical applications.
